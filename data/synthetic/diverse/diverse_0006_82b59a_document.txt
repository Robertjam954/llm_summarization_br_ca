TECHNIQUE:
Real-time grayscale and color Doppler ultrasound of the left breast and left axilla was
performed using a high-frequency linear array transducer (7–15 MHz).

FINDINGS:

LEFT BREAST:
A large, irregular, hypoechoic solid mass was identified in the left breast at the 10 o'clock
position, approximately 3.5 cm from the nipple, in the anterior-to-mid depth. The mass
measures 3.8 x 3.1 x 2.9 cm (AP x transverse x craniocaudal). Margins are spiculated and

CLINICAL HISTORY:
Left breast 10 o'clock mass, BI-RADS 5 on ultrasound, biopsy pending. MRI requested for
extent of disease evaluation prior to surgical planning.

TECHNIQUE:
Bilateral breast MRI was performed on a 3.0 Tesla MRI scanner using a dedicated 16-channel
breast coil. Sequences included T1-weighted fat-saturated pre- and post-contrast series,
T2-weighted STIR, and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI). Gadolinium-based contrast agent
(gadobutrol, 0.1 mmol/kg) was administered intravenously.

FINDINGS:

LEFT BREAST:
There is a large, irregular mass in the upper outer quadrant of the left breast at the
10 o'clock position, anterior-to-mid depth, approximately 3.5 cm from the nipple. The mass
demonstrates heterogeneous, rim-enhancing kinetics with initial rapid enhancement and delayed
washout (type III curve), consistent with highly suspicious morphology. On post-contrast
subtraction images, the mass measures 4.1 x 3.5 x 3.3 cm (AP x transverse x craniocaudal),
slightly larger than the ultrasound estimate given improved soft tissue delineation. Spiculated
margins are confirmed. Restricted diffusion is noted on DWI (low ADC value, approximately
0.82 x 10-3 mm2/s).

ADDITIONAL ENHANCEMENT — LEFT BREAST:
There is an area of clumped, segmental non-mass enhancement (NME) in the upper outer
quadrant of the left breast extending approximately 2.5 cm posterior-to-lateral to the index
mass, with similar washout kinetics. This finding raises concern for additional sites of
in-breast disease involvement and will require further evaluation.

No additional suspicious enhancing lesions are identified in the left breast beyond those
described above.

RIGHT BREAST:
No suspicious mass, non-mass enhancement, or architectural distortion identified. Scattered
fibroglandular tissue. BI-RADS 1 right breast.

LEFT AXILLA:
Multiple left axillary lymph nodes with diffuse cortical thickening (up to 8 mm) and loss of
fatty hila are again noted, consistent with suspicious nodal involvement previously described
on ultrasound.

RIGHT AXILLA: No pathologically enlarged lymph nodes.

SKIN AND CHEST WALL: No skin thickening, skin retraction, or chest wall involvement identified.

IMPRESSION:
1. Left breast upper outer quadrant, 10 o'clock, anterior-to-mid depth: 4.1 cm irregular,
   rim-enhancing mass — highly suspicious for malignancy. BI-RADS CATEGORY 5.
2. Associated clumped segmental non-mass enhancement in the left upper outer quadrant,
   extending 2.5 cm posterior-to-lateral to the index mass — suspicious; may represent
   additional extent of disease.
3. Abnormal left axillary lymph nodes, suspicious for nodal metastatic disease.
4. Right breast: BI-RADS 1 — negative.

RECOMMENDATION: Proceed with tissue sampling (ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy) of the
index left breast mass as previously recommended. MRI-guided biopsy of the additional non-mass
enhancement region should be considered if pathology dictates. Surgical oncology referral
recommended upon pathologic confirmation of malignancy.

PROCEDURE REPORT:

INDICATION: Left breast 10 o'clock, BI-RADS 5 mass, 3.8 cm on ultrasound, 4.1 cm on MRI.
Biopsy for histologic diagnosis.

INFORMED CONSENT: Obtained. Risks, benefits, and alternatives discussed with the patient

PROCEDURE DETAIL:
The patient was positioned supine with the left arm elevated. The left breast mass at the
10 o'clock position was re-identified under real-time ultrasound guidance. The overlying skin
was prepped and draped in sterile fashion. Local anesthesia was achieved with 1% lidocaine
with epinephrine, approximately 5 mL, infiltrated along the planned biopsy track.

Using a 14-gauge automated spring-loaded core biopsy needle (Bard Magnum system), multiple
passes were made through the mass under continuous real-time ultrasound visualization.
A total of five (5) core specimens were obtained and submitted in formalin for histopathologic
analysis. Satisfactory tissue was confirmed by visual inspection of the cores.

NOTE: No biopsy clip/marker was placed at the conclusion of the procedure. The decision was
made in discussion with the patient, who is proceeding directly to surgical planning given the
size and imaging characteristics of the lesion.

POST-PROCEDURE: Hemostasis was achieved with manual compression. A sterile dressing was
applied. Post-procedure mammogram was not performed given that no clip was deployed.
Patient tolerated the procedure well without immediate complication.
