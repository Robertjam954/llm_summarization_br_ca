ULTRASOUND — LEFT BREAST AND LEFT AXILLA
Examination Date: 03/14/2023
Technologist: C. Morales, RDMS
Interpreting Radiologist: Angela S. Firth, MD

CLINICAL HISTORY:
52-year-old female presenting with palpable left breast mass at the 10 o'clock position. No prior breast imaging on file at this institution.

TECHNIQUE:
High-resolution grayscale and color Doppler ultrasound of the left breast and left axilla was performed using a linear array transducer (7–15 MHz).

FINDINGS:

LEFT BREAST — FOCUS 1 (INDEX LESION):
At the 10 o'clock position of the left breast, approximately 3.5 cm from the nipple, there is an irregular, hypoechoic mass measuring 2.8 x 2.1 x 2.4 cm. The mass demonstrates
posterior acoustic shadowing, angular margins, and marked internal vascularity on Doppler imaging. The lesion is located in the anterior-to-mid depth of the breast tissue.

LEFT BREAST — FOCUS 2 (SATELLITE LESION):
Immediately posterior and superior to the index lesion, at the 10–11 o'clock junction approximately 4.2 cm from the nipple, there is a second hypoechoic irregular mass
meas uring 0.9 x 0.7 x 0.8 cm. This satellite lesion demonstrates similar morphologic features including angular margins and internal vascularity, raising concern for multifocal
disease.

CALCIFICATIONS:
No definitive calcifications were identified on ultrasound; however, this modality is limited for calcification detection.

LEFT AXILLA:
An abnormal left axillary lymph node was identified at level I. The node measures 2.1 x 1.4 cm and demonstrates cortical thickening of 7 mm with loss of the normal fatty
hilum, highly suspicious for metastatic involvement.

IMPRESSION:
1. Two irregular hypoechoic masses in the left breast at the 10 o'clock position, measuring 2.8 cm (index) and 0.9 cm (satellite), highly suspicious for multifocal malignancy.
   BI-RADS 5.
2. Morphologically abnormal left axillary lymph node with cortical thickening and loss of fatty hilum, suspicious for nodal metastasis.

RECOMMENDATION: MRI left breast for further extent-of-disease evaluation. Ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy of the dominant mass and the suspicious axillary lymph node
is recommended.

BREAST MRI — LEFT BREAST
Examination Date: 03/20/2023
Interpreting Radiologist: Angela S. Firth, MD

CLINICAL HISTORY:
52-year-old female with BI-RADS 5 findings on left breast ultrasound. MRI performed for extent-of-disease evaluation prior to surgical planning.

TECHNIQUE:
Bilateral breast MRI performed on a 3.0 Tesla system (Siemens Vida) with dedicated breast coil. Sequences included axial T1 pre-contrast, axial T2 fat-saturated, diffusion-weighted
imaging (DWI/ADC), and dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) series post intravenous administration of gadobutrol 0.1 mmol/kg. CAD analysis was applied.

FINDINGS:

LEFT BREAST — INDEX MASS (FOCUS 1):
At the 10 o'clock position, anterior-to-mid depth, 3.5 cm from the nipple, there is a heterogeneously enhancing irregular mass measuring 3.0 x 2.3 x 2.6 cm on MRI (slightly
larger than sonographic measurement). The mass demonstrates type III (washout) kinetic curve. Restricted diffusion is present (ADC approximately 0.78 x 10⁻³ mm²/s).

LEFT BREAST — SATELLITE LESION (FOCUS 2):
At the 10–11 o'clock position, 4.2 cm from the nipple, a second enhancing irregular mass measuring 1.0 x 0.8 x 0.9 cm is confirmed, with similar washout kinetics. The two
lesions are separated by approximately 1.5 cm of intervening breast tissue, consistent with multifocal disease within the same quadrant.

ADDITIONAL ENHANCEMENT — NON-MASS ENHANCEMENT (NME):
There is segmental non-mass enhancement extending along the ductal axis from the retroareolar region toward the index mass, spanning approximately 4.0 cm in craniocaudal
extent. This NME is clumped in internal pattern and is suspicious for an extensive intraductal component or DCIS. No abnormal enhancement is identified in the right breast.

AXILLARY LYMPH NODES:
The previously identified left axillary level I lymph node demonstrates avid heterogeneous enhancement with cortical thickening, corroborating the suspicious ultrasound
findings. No contralateral axillary adenopathy identified.

CALCIFICATIONS/ASYMMETRY:
No suspicious asymmetry on MRI. Calcifications are not well-characterized by MRI; mammographic correlation was recommended (see separate report addendum).

SKIN/CHEST WALL:
No skin thickening or chest wall invasion identified.

IMPRESSION:
1. Multifocal left breast malignancy: dominant mass 3.0 cm and satellite mass 1.0 cm at 10 o'clock, with segmental NME (clumped pattern, ~4.0 cm extent) extending
   retroareolar, consistent with extensive DCIS component. BI-RADS 6 (biopsy proven).
2. Suspicious left axillary level I lymphadenopathy.
3. No right breast abnormality.

RECOMMENDATION: Surgical oncology consultation. Completion of tissue sampling including US-guided biopsy of dominant mass, satellite lesion, and left axillary lymph node
as clinically indicated. Tumor board review recommended.

ULTRASOUND-GUIDED CORE NEEDLE BIOPSY — LEFT BREAST (DOMINANT MASS AND AXILLARY LYMPH NODE)
Procedure Date: 03/24/2023
Performing Radiologist: Angela S. Firth, MD
Procedure Nurse: T. Benson, RN

PROCEDURE NOTE:

INFORMED CONSENT: Obtained. Risks, benefits, and alternatives discussed with patient.

SITE 1 — LEFT BREAST DOMINANT MASS (10 O'CLOCK, 3.5 CM FROM NIPPLE):
Under real-time ultrasound guidance, the skin was prepped and draped in sterile fashion. Local anesthesia was achieved with 1% lidocaine without epinephrine. Using a
14-gauge automated core needle (Bard Mission), five (5) passes were made through the index mass at 10 o'clock, 3.5 cm from the nipple, anterior-to-mid depth. Adequate
tissue cores were obtained and submitted in 10% neutral buffered formalin. A titanium biopsy clip (HydroMARK, omega shape) was deployed at the biopsy site under
ultrasound visualization and confirmed to be in satisfactory position within the lesion. Post-procedure mammographic clip localization images were obtained confirming clip
placement within the targeted mass.

SITE 2 — LEFT AXILLARY LYMPH NODE (LEVEL I):
Using real-time ultrasound guidance and the same sterile preparation, a 22-gauge fine needle aspiration (FNA) of the suspicious left level I axillary lymph node was performed.
Three passes were made; material was expressed onto glass slides and submitted for cytologic evaluation. A separate titanium clip was not placed in the lymph node.

COMPLICATIONS: None. Patient tolerated procedure well. Post-procedure instructions provided.

─── SPECIMEN A — LEFT BREAST CORE BIOPSY ───

GROSS DESCRIPTION:
Specimen A consists of five tan-white cylindrical core biopsy fragments ranging from 0.9 to 1.5 cm in length. Submitted entirely in one cassette.

MICROSCOPIC DESCRIPTION:
Sections show cores of breast parenchyma infiltrated by an invasive carcinoma with high nuclear grade (grade 3). The invasive component consists of nests and sheets of
pleomorphic epithelial cells with abundant mitotic figures (>10 per 10 HPF), prominent nucleoli, and central tumor necrosis. No tubule formation is identified (tubule score
3). Nuclear pleomorphism score 3. Mitotic score 3. Overall Elston-Ellis Modified Scarff-Bloom-Richardson histologic grade: Grade 3 (score 9/9).

In addition, extensive high-grade ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) is present within and adjacent to the invasive component. The DCIS is of solid and comedo subtypes with
central comedo-type necrosis. Nuclear grade: high (grade 3).

The invasive component measures at least 2.8 cm in greatest dimension on core biopsy (correlating with the imaging index mass of 3.0 cm by MRI). Assessment of final
invasive component size will be confirmed at surgical excision.

LYMPHOVASCULAR INVASION: Present (identified within core biopsy sections).

DIAGNOSIS — SPECIMEN A:
Left breast, 10 o'clock, US-guided core needle biopsy:
INVASIVE DUCTAL CARCINOMA (IDC), NOT OTHERWISE SPECIFIED (NOS), HISTOLOGIC GRADE 3
  — with extensive HIGH-GRADE DUCTAL CARCINOMA IN SITU (DCIS), solid and comedo subtypes
  — Invasive component size on core biopsy: at least 2.8 cm
  — Lymphovascular invasion: Present

─── SPECIMEN B — LEFT AXILLARY LYMPH NODE FNA ───

CYTOLOGIC DESCRIPTION:
Smears and cell block sections show clusters and single epithelial cells with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios, irregular nuclear membranes, prominent nucleoli, and
intracytoplasmic mucin vacuoles. Features are consistent with metastatic carcinoma. Lymphocytes are present in background.

DIAGNOSIS — SPECIMEN B:
Left axillary lymph node, level I, fine needle aspiration:
POSITIVE FOR METASTATIC CARCINOMA, consistent with metastatic breast carcinoma.

COMMENT: Correlation with clinical and imaging findings is recommended. Multifocal disease noted on imaging; tissue from the satellite lesion was not sampled at this
biopsy encounter. Surgical staging will be required for definitive nodal and extent assessment.
